# Blueprint: The Minimap — Terrain, Fog, Dots, Camera Frustum, Interaction and Alerts (B-204, the minimap half of B-183)

The client has no minimap. **B-204** (`DESIGN.md` §4) records the cost: StarCraft II 5.0.14 shows an attacker's
snapshot "in fog and on minimap" (plan [`2026-11-24-attacker-snapshot`](2026-11-24-attacker-snapshot.md) S5), and
plan `2026-11-24-attacker-snapshot` fixed only the battlefield half of **B-183**. That plan published
`PlayerView::attack_snapshots` so that a later minimap could draw it (its §14). Q0112 part 1 answered (a): close the
battlefield half and keep the minimap half as B-204 until a minimap is built. This plan builds that minimap. It is
a complete StarCraft II style minimap, not only a carrier for B-183's snapshots, because an experienced player
misses every part of it at once:

- terrain with its cliff levels;
- the viewer's fog of war;
- own, allied and enemy units and structures as player-coloured squares sized by footprint;
- resources;
- the camera's view trapezoid;
- attack alerts, pings and B-183's attacker snapshots;
- rally lines of the selected structures.

The left button moves the camera. The right button issues the same smart command a right click in the world issues.

**The design in one paragraph.** A new client module, `client::minimap`, keeps one retained **minimap frame** in
the main world. On a new published tick or a selection change, it rebuilds an ordered list of **marks** from the
presented `RenderSnapshot`, the viewer's `BattleFog` (the same `presence_of` rule as the world) and the map's
resource billboards. Each mark is a square, a ring or a segment in minimap space. Every frame it computes the camera
frustum's ground trapezoid (four ray–plane hits). A top-level render-graph node, `MinimapNode`, runs before Bevy's
camera driver and so before egui's window pass (bevy_egui 0.33 `render_systems.rs:134`). It draws into one
retained `Image` with two pipelines of one new shader, `assets/shaders/minimap.wgsl`:
- a full-screen triangle that shades terrain and fog from the map's cell texture, the terrain's own splat texture
  and the theme's real albedo arrays, read at their 1×1 mip, and a fog texture;
- an instanced quad pass that draws the marks from a storage buffer.

egui shows that image in a new left column of the command panel, at the bottom left as in StarCraft II, and turns
pointer input on it into a `MinimapIntent`. The intent moves the camera, or runs through the battle input's order
path with a target picked on the minimap, not along a world ray. Nothing in the simulation changes.

### 2.2 The rules that follow

1. **A minimap is a terrain image darkened toward black by fog, with coloured quads over it** (S2, S3). Fog never
   hides a quad in the shader: what is drawn is decided before, by what the player may see [INF].
2. **Dots are at least 3 px** and have a 1 px black border (S5). Their half-width is `MinimapRadius`, which is the
   radius for units and about half the footprint for structures (S6) [INF].
3. **Resources are light blue** (S5 `ResourceUnitColor`), the **frustum is white** (S5), and the background is
   black (S5).
4. **Colours.** Each player's colour by default, or the team colours of S8 when toggled (`ColorButton`, S9). A unit
   struck within the last 3 s is drawn in `Attacked` red (S8) [INF].
5. **Attack alerts** (S4) come in two kinds: base (a structure struck) and forces (a unit struck). Each pings red at
   the struck unit's position for `PingTime` and stays in the alert list for `Life`. A new one is not raised while,
   within the last `OverlapDuration`, two alerts were raised anywhere or one within `OverlapLocalRadius` of it.
6. **Space** jumps the camera to the latest alert still alive and cycles back through older ones (S10).
7. **The minimap takes orders** (S11): a right click on it issues the smart command at that point (rule 8), a
   command mode aims at the point, and Shift queues. A left click with no mode moves the camera and never selects
   (common knowledge of the game, **verify**).
8. **A right click on a resource's square** is a harvest (S11's mineral-patch transfers, **verify** that it
   resolves on the minimap and not only in the world). On a unit's square it targets the unit (**verify**). D-7
   follows both.

### 2.3 Time base of S4 and S8

5.0.14's data times are Normal game seconds. This project runs 25 ticks per Faster second (Q0052 S-5), and one
Faster second is 1.4 Normal seconds, so a Normal second is 25 / 1.4 ticks. `GameUIData`'s millisecond values are UI
timers, taken as real milliseconds at Faster (**verify**). The data file authors ticks, with this arithmetic in its
comment:

| Value | 5.0.14 | Ticks |
|---|---|---:|
| Attack alert ping (`PingTime`) | 6 Normal s | 107 |
| Attack alert life (`Life`) | 15 Normal s | 268 |
| Overlap window (`OverlapDuration`) | 15 Normal s | 268 |
| Overlap radius (`OverlapLocalRadius`) | 15 world units | 15.0 (world units) |
| Overlap counts (`OverlapGlobalCount`, `OverlapLocalCount`) | 2, 1 | — |
| Self ping (default `PingTime`) | 6 Normal s | 107 |
| Struck flash (`UnitDamageFlashDuration`) | 3,000 ms | 75 |

Alerts run on the **presented simulation tick**, not on wall time. A paused game holds its pings, alerts are
deterministic in tests, and the speed controls scale them as they scale the battle.

## 4. Design decisions

### D-1. Rendering: a retained render-graph node with two instanced pipelines, shown through egui

**Chosen.** `MinimapNode` is a top-level render-graph node with an edge `MinimapNode → CameraDriverLabel`. It
records **one render pass** into the minimap `Image`'s texture (`RenderAssets<GpuImage>`):
1. **Terrain and fog:** one full-screen triangle, entry points `terrain_vs` and `terrain_fs` of
   `assets/shaders/minimap.wgsl`, drawn over the whole target. It shades the map rectangle (D-3, D-4) and the
   letterbox in the background colour.
2. **Marks:** `draw(0..6, 0..count)` of entry points `mark_vs` and `mark_fs`. Each instance is one `MinimapMark`
   from a storage buffer (D-5): a square with a border, a ring or a segment, with its shape chosen by a `kind`
   field and drawn as a signed-distance quad.
3. **Frustum:** four more instances of the same pipeline, `draw(0..6, 0..4)`, which read the trapezoid's corners
   from the uniform (D-8). They need no buffer write when only the camera moved.

egui shows the image with `egui::Image` of the `TextureId` from `EguiContexts::add_image`, taken once per minimap
image. The image is an `Image` asset created with `RENDER_ATTACHMENT | TEXTURE_BINDING | COPY_SRC` usages and
`RenderAssetUsages::RENDER_WORLD`, at the column's size in physical pixels. It is recreated only when that size
changes, which happens on a window or scale change, never in the steady state.

**Uploads.** The main world's `MinimapFrame` is extracted into the render world with `clone_from` into retained
`Vec`s. In `RenderSet::Prepare` the node's resource writes:
- the uniform (layout, frustum corners, presented tick and colour mode) every frame;
- the marks with `RenderQueue::write_buffer` only when the frame's `marks_generation` moved, into a storage buffer
  that doubles its capacity when outgrown (growth, not steady state);
- the fog texture (`R8Unorm`, map width × height) with `RenderQueue::write_texture` only when the fog's `refills`
  moved;
- the cell texture (`Rgba8Unorm`) only when `TerrainState::revision` or the session moved.

No Bevy asset is modified per frame, so Bevy's `Image` re-extraction, which clones the bytes, never runs for these
textures.

**Why not an egui painter.** egui would tessellate up to several hundred shapes every frame on the CPU, each through
egui's own allocating mesh builders. The fog would be a CPU-generated texture handed to egui as an `ImageDelta`,
which allocates the full image on every tick. The theme's albedo arrays cannot be reached from egui, so the terrain
would be a CPU-baked colour image instead of the real textures. And none of it would be our shader: the charter's
real-shader rule (§1.2) would be met only by egui's generic one. It is rejected on §3.3 (allocations, O(N) CPU
tessellation every frame) and §1.2.

**Why not a second camera with `Material2d`.** A camera costs a view, its own uniforms, visibility and phase
sorting every frame. Bevy's `Material2d` has no instancing, so the marks would need a custom phase item anyway, and
the ordering of an image camera against egui's window pass would have to be held by camera `order` rather than by
one graph edge. A top-down orthographic render of the terrain mesh (StarCraft II bakes its minimap from the terrain,
S2) is also rejected. Lighting decides how cliff levels read, vertical cliff walls vanish from above, it needs a
camera lifecycle at every map load, and its pixels cannot be predicted exactly for tests.

**"GPU point instancing"** is what the marks pass is. WebGPU points are always 1 px, so each instance is a
six-vertex quad.

**Cost.** Two pipelines are compiled once and queued at startup through `PipelineCache`. A frame costs one uniform
write, one pass with one full-screen triangle and two instanced draws, at most about 1,000 instances (§6 M-B2).

**Where the CPU work runs.** `prepare_minimap_frame` runs in `BattleSet::Present` after `camera_control_system` and
needs no renderer and no egui. So the `MinimalPlugins` children of the allocation tests can run it (T15). The egui
part is drawing only.

### D-2. Coordinate spaces and the mapping

`MinimapLayout` (pure, in `minimap/layout.rs`) is built from the map's size in cells `(w, h)` and the target's side
in physical pixels `side`:
- `scale = side / max(w, h)` pixels per world unit;
- `offset = ((side − w·scale) / 2, (side − h·scale) / 2)`, which letterboxes a map that is not square, as StarCraft
  II's black background does (S5).

| Function | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| `to_minimap(world: Vec2) -> Vec2` | sim `(x, y)` = Bevy `(x, z)`, world units | pixels from the target's top-left: `offset + world · scale`. Screen-up is sim −y in both the world view and the minimap (T8), so no axis flips |
| `to_world(px: Vec2) -> Vec2` | pixels | world units, the inverse |
| `to_world_clamped(px) -> Vec2` | pixels | the inverse, clamped to `[0.5, w − 0.5] × [0.5, h − 0.5]`, the camera's own clamp (T8), so a click in the letterbox aims at the nearest map edge |
| `contains(px) -> bool` | pixels | whether the pixel lies on the map, not in the letterbox |

The egui response's position, in points, becomes pixels by subtracting the image's `rect.min` and multiplying by
`pixels_per_point`. The shader receives `scale`, `offset` and `side` in the uniform, so CPU and GPU use one mapping.
Unit tests cover the round trip, both corners, the letterbox of a non-square map and the clamp (M-1).

### D-3. Terrain with cliff levels (WP1)

- **Cell texture** (`minimap/terrain.rs`, built from `GameMap` on the CPU once per session or terrain revision, into
  a retained `Vec<u8>`). One RGBA8 texel per cell:
  - R: the cell's level, as `level / CliffLevel::MAX` for a flat cell, or the ramp's height at the cell centre over
    `3 · CLIFF_STEP` (`RampRun::height_at`), so a ramp shades smoothly from one landing to the other;
  - G: 255 when `GROUND_PATHABLE` and not `Abyss`;
  - B: 255 when some 4-neighbour is on a lower level, meaning the high side of a cliff edge;
  - A: 255.
  It is read with `textureLoad`, one texel per cell, so edges stay sharp at any size.
- **Ground colour** in `terrain_fs`: a splat-weighted sum of the theme's albedo layers. Each layer is sampled with
  `textureSampleLevel` at its **last mip**, a 1×1 linear average of the real baked texture (T12). The weights are the
  existing splat `Image`, sampled at the cell centre. The cliff layer colours edges. No new art is authored.
- **Level brightness:** the ground colour times `level_brightness[level]` (data, D-9), interpolated on ramps.
  Unpathable ground is `unpathable_color`. Cliff-edge texels are mixed toward the cliff layer's colour by
  `edge_mix`.
- Outside the map, the shader writes `background` (S5 black).
- **Terrain toggle** (S9 `TerrainButton`, D-10 key). When off, the map rectangle is `background` and only fog,
  marks and frustum are drawn, as StarCraft II's toggle hides the terrain image.

### D-4. Fog of war (WP2)

- `BattleFog` keeps `cells: Vec<VisibilityState>` (one byte each) instead of `visible: Vec<bool>`. `sees()` keeps
  its meaning (`== Visible`), and `retained_buffers` and `buffers` rename `fog.visible` to `fog.cells`. A new
  `cell_states(&self) -> &[VisibilityState]` exposes the slice. The refill stays allocation-free.
- The minimap's fog bytes (`R8Unorm`, row-major like the view) are `fog_darkening[state]` from data. The shipped
  values are `Visible 0`, `Fogged 0.45` and `Unexplored 0.85`, an art choice (Q0020) for WP5's review, since the
  engine's `p_vFOWLevels` are not in the data (S2). The bytes are rebuilt from `cell_states` only when the fog's
  `refills` count moved (T4), into the retained vector.
- `terrain_fs` samples the fog **bilinearly**, so fog edges are soft, as S2's filtered texture is, and computes
  `color = mix(color, background, fog)`. This is S2's lerp to black.
- **The fog never hides a mark in the shader** (rule 1). What is listed is already what the viewer may see (D-5).
  So the fog test is two tests: a CPU test that no enemy mark is listed in fog (M-4), and a GPU test that a fogged
  cell is darker than a seen one (G-3).

### D-5. Marks: what is drawn, how big, in what colour (WP2)

```rust
/// One shape on the minimap, in minimap pixels (plan `2026-12-01-minimap` D-5). Packed for the
/// storage buffer the marks pass reads.
#[repr(C)]
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, bytemuck::Pod, bytemuck::Zeroable)]
pub struct MinimapMark {
    /// Centre (square, ring) or start point (segment), pixels from the target's top-left.
    pub a: [f32; 2],
    /// Half-width and half-height (square), outer radius and thickness (ring), or end point (segment).
    pub b: [f32; 2],
    /// Linear RGBA, premultiplied in the shader.
    pub color: [f32; 4],
    /// `MarkKind` as a number; ring marks carry their start tick for the pulse.
    pub kind: u32,
    /// Presented tick the mark's animation starts at (rings), else 0.
    pub start_tick: u32,
    /// Border width in pixels (squares) or line width (segments).
    pub stroke: f32,
    pub _pad: f32,
}
```

`prepare_minimap_frame` rebuilds the marks when the presented tick, the fog's `refills`, the selection's generation,
the colour mode or the layout changed, and never otherwise. It clears the retained `Vec` and pushes, in draw order:

1. **Resources:** each resource record of `MapPresentation::objects` whose `BattleFog::node_present` says it was last
   seen present. Square half-width: the node's footprint half-extent, from the map's `ResourceNode` footprint
   (**verify** the field; otherwise the authored billboard `size` / 2 from `map_entity_visuals.ron`). Colour:
   `resource_color` (S5 `126,191,241`). Watchtowers: `watchtower_color`, half-width `watchtower_half`.
2. **Structures, then units:** each bubble of `presented().bubbles` for which `presence_of(fog, bubble)` is
   `Presence::Shown` and whose archetype is `selectable` and `order_targetable` (a hangar member is drawn by
   neither, as it is never picked: the same capability test as `BattleView::can_pick`). Therefore:
   - no enemy in fog;
   - no undetected burrowed enemy;
   - no undetected cloaked enemy (its shimmer is a world effect, never a minimap square; **verify**: 5.0.14 shows
     nothing);
   - no housed harvester;
   - an attack-revealed enemy is drawn.

   Half-width: `max(footprint_w, footprint_h) / 2` for a `BuildingDome`, the bubble's `radius` otherwise (S6),
   then at least `min_dot_px / 2` (S5's 3 px). Barracks-like 3×3 structures draw 1.5, against 5.0.14's 1.75: a
   detail difference, recorded in D-11. Border: `border_px` of `border_color` (S5: 1 px black). Structures are drawn
   before units so that a unit stays visible on top of a base.
3. **Attack snapshots** (WP4, D-6).
4. **Rally lines** (WP4, D-12).
5. **Alert and ping rings** (WP4, D-13).

**Colour.** `ColorMode::Player` (default) uses `PlayerPalette`'s linear colour of the owner, and the neutral colour
for `PlayerId::NEUTRAL`. `ColorMode::Team` uses S8's `SelfMinimap`, `Ally`, `Hostile` and `Neutral` diffuse colours.
The project has no teams, so every other player counts as hostile. A team model, when one lands, maps allies to
`Ally` with no change here. In team mode one's own **selected** units take `Selected` (S8). Any unit struck within
`flash_ticks` takes `attacked_color` in both modes (S8 `Attacked`, D-13).

**Cost.** O(N) in the bubbles per rebuild, plus O(nodes), with one O(1) catalog lookup per bubble. No pair loop. The
retained `Vec` keeps its capacity, so a rebuild at or below the high-water mark allocates nothing. A
`// PERFORMANCE RATIONALE:` block on the builder states why the list is rebuilt per tick and not diffed: the list is
at most about 1,000 marks of 48 bytes; a diff costs more bookkeeping than it saves; and the upload is one
`write_buffer` per tick.

### D-6. B-183's minimap half: the attacker snapshot (WP4, closes B-204)

Each `BattleFog::attack_snapshots()` entry gets one square at `bubble.world_pos`, sized and coloured from its
`bubble` exactly as a live unit is (D-5). The view publishes exactly what the viewer keeps (T6), so:
- the square appears on the tick the image appears;
- it is dropped on the tick the view drops it: sight, the attacker seen, or Q0112 2(c)'s 25-tick cap;
- it is never listed for an attacker the view also shows (attacker-snapshot As built deviation 1).

The square is undimmed by default (S12 says only "appear … on minimap"). `snapshot_alpha` in data (default 1.0)
lets WP5's review dim it if the look asks for it (**verify**). A snapshot is never picked on the minimap (D-7), as in
the world.

### D-7. Interaction (WP3)

**Input ownership.** The minimap is an egui widget inside the command panel, so `sample_input_ownership` already
denies the world the pointer over it (T2). No world gesture can start on it, and egui's drag capture keeps a drag
that began on it there until release. `draw_minimap` (in `panels.rs`, or `minimap/panel.rs` called from it) reads
the `egui::Response` of an `Image` with `Sense::click_and_drag()` and the frame's pointer and modifiers, and writes a
`MinimapIntent`:

```rust
/// What the pointer did on the minimap this frame, in world units (plan `2026-12-01-minimap` D-7).
#[derive(Resource, Debug, Default, Clone, Copy, PartialEq)]
pub struct MinimapIntent {
    /// Left button pressed or dragged with no command mode: centre the camera here.
    pub look_at: Option<Vec2>,
    /// Left press with a command mode armed: aim the mode here.
    pub aim_left: Option<Vec2>,
    /// Right press: the smart command (or cancel a mode) here.
    pub aim_right: Option<Vec2>,
    /// Alt + left press: ping here (D-13).
    pub ping: Option<Vec2>,
    /// Shift held at the press.
    pub shift: bool,
}
```

Right and left act on the **press**, as in the world, which reads `just_pressed`.

**Camera.** `apply_minimap_intent` runs in `CollectIntent` after `battle_hud_system` and before
`collect_pointer_intent`. It sets `RtsCamera::target` to `look_at`; `camera_control_system` clamps and eases the
height in `Present` as it already does. A jump during a middle-button camera drag cancels that drag
(`CameraDragState`'s cancellation, T8 module), so the two never fight.

**Orders: one path for the world and the minimap.** `battle_input/mod.rs` is refactored, with no behaviour change,
so that "where the pointer aims" is separate from "what order follows":
- `PointerAim { unit: Option<PickedUnit>, node: Option<(ResourceNodeId, Vec3)>, ground: Option<Vec3> }` is what a
  press found. `BattleView::aim_along(ray)` builds it from a ray, which is today's `pick_unit`, `pick_node` and
  `ground_point`. `minimap::pick::aim_at(world_point)` builds it from the minimap:
  - the topmost listed unit or structure square containing the point, in reverse draw order, filtered by
    `BattleView::can_pick`, so never a snapshot;
  - else the resource square containing it;
  - and always the ground at that point, at `terrain_altitude_scalar` height.
- `issue_pointer_order(aim, …)` and `handle_targeting_pointer(aim, …)` take a `PointerAim` instead of a ray.
  `pointer_target(aim)` keeps `resolve_target`'s precedence. Whatever turns a target into orders on `main` at the
  time (today `context_order` and the rally rule; after `rallies-plan` WP1, `smart_plan` and `submit_smart`) is
  then shared by both sources with no second copy. Whichever of the two plans lands second rebases onto the other's
  function signature; neither touches the other's table.

| Minimap press | Mode | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Left, no modifier | Normal | Camera centred on the point; the selection is unchanged (rule 7) |
| Left | Point mode (`AttackMove`, move, patrol, `CastAtPoint`, `Rally`) | The mode's order at the aim, exactly as a world click: A-mode on a picked square attacks that unit (`submit_attack_on_unit`); `Rally` rallies on the aim's target; Shift keeps the mode |
| Left | `CastOnUnit` | The picked square's unit, or `NoCastTarget` |
| Right | Normal, selection not empty, orders open | The world's right click with the aim's target (enemy square, friendly square, node square, ground): smart order and rally; Shift queues; the order marker shows in the world at the target's ground (rule 8) |
| Right | Any armed mode | The mode is cancelled, as in the world |
| Alt + left | Any | A ping (D-13); no camera move |
| Any | Match over, or no battle | Left still moves the camera; nothing orders (the world's rule, T9 module invariant) |

Picking on the minimap is O(N) in the listed marks per press, through the retained list, with no allocation. It
runs on a press, never per frame.

### D-8. The camera frustum (WP3)

`minimap/frustum.rs` has `ground_trapezoid(camera: &Camera, transform: &GlobalTransform, viewport: Rect, plane_y:
f32) -> Option<[Vec2; 4]>`. It casts the four corner rays of the camera's **viewport** (the world rectangle, not
the window: T2), through `Camera::viewport_to_world`, onto the plane `y = look_at_y`. That is the height the camera
looks at, so the trapezoid's centre is where a minimap click puts the camera. It returns the four `(x, z)` hits in
the order top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left. A corner ray that does not meet the plane in front of the
camera, or meets it farther than `frustum_max_distance` (data), is cut at that distance along its ray's ground
projection. With the 58° pitch and the 48-unit zoom cap that never happens (**verify** at WP3, M-5); the cut keeps
the shape finite if a future camera allows it.

The corners go to the uniform every frame. The marks pass draws four segments between them in `frustum_color` (S5
white), `frustum_px` wide, scissored to the map rectangle so that a view past the edge is cut there. The cost is
four ray–plane hits and 64 bytes of uniform per frame.

### D-11. Recorded differences from 5.0.14 (details, Q0051 refinement)

- A 3×3 structure's square half-width is 1.5, not 1.75 (S6). The footprint rule replaces a per-type authored
  `MinimapRadius`, which would need a catalog field the simulation crates parse. Detail; recorded in the plan and
  in `DESIGN.md`'s HUD row, not a bug row.
- An attack alert is raised by the strike's **visual event**, not by the damage itself (T10). A projectile's alert
  comes at launch, one flight time early, and damage with no strike event (spell damage over time) raises none.
  Registered as **B-248** (deferred): fixing it needs a per-viewer "struck" list in `PlayerView`, a simulation
  presentation change this plan does not make.
- No teams exist, so pings are seen by their own player only, and team mode has no ally colour in use. This follows
  the project's team model, not a minimap choice.

### D-12. Rally lines (WP4, "if cheap": it is)

For each **selected** own structure (the `BattleSelection` ids), the view's `RallyView` points of that entity, in
`(slot, index)` order (T6), give segments: structure position → point 0 → point 1 …, in `rally_color`, `rally_px`
wide. The structure's position is looked up in the presented bubbles by entity. They are sorted by `entity_id`
(**verify**; if not, the builder keeps a retained `(entity, index)` vector sorted once per rebuild, O(N log N) per
tick). The cost is O(selected structures × waypoints) per rebuild. Order paths of selected **units** (S7's
`DefaultPath`) are not drawn in the world either. They are registered as **B-249** (deferred) and are out of this
plan's scope.

### D-13. Alerts, pings and the struck flash (WP4)

`minimap/alerts.rs` owns `AlertLog`, a resource of three retained, bounded structures:
- `alerts: Vec<Alert { kind: Town | Unit, at: Vec2, tick: u32 }>`, the live alerts newest last, pruned when older
  than `life_ticks`;
- `pings: Vec<Ping { at, tick, color }>`, pruned after `ping_ticks`;
- `struck: Vec<(EntityId, u32)>`, sorted by entity, each viewer unit's last struck tick, pruned after
  `flash_ticks`.

`collect_alerts` runs in `PreparePresentation`. It reads `CombatVisualEvent`s, and for each strike event whose
`ends.target` is owned by the viewer (looked up in the presented bubbles) it:
1. upserts `struck` (binary search);
2. decides the kind: `Town` when the target's shape is `BuildingDome`, else `Unit` (S4 `AttackTown` and
   `AttackUnit`);
3. raises an alert unless, among alerts of either kind raised within `overlap_ticks`, at least `overlap_global`
   exist, or at least `overlap_local` lie within `overlap_radius` of the struck position (S4, rule 5). The test is
   O(live alerts), bounded by `overlap_global` + those still alive. No pair loop over units;
4. on a raised alert, pushes a ping at the position with `attack.color` and one feed line (`text_town` or
   `text_unit`) through `BattleFeed`'s notice path (**verify** its API).

**Bounds.** At most `overlap_global` alerts are raised per `overlap_ticks`, so `alerts` and `pings` hold at most a
handful. `struck` holds at most the viewer's units. All three keep their capacity.

**Space** (`ALERT_JUMP_KEY`) sets `RtsCamera::target` to the newest live alert. Each further press within
`life_ticks` steps to the next older one, and wraps (S10). The cursor resets when a new alert is raised.

**Ping** (D-10) pushes a ping with `ping.color` at the point.

**Marks.** Each live ping is a ring mark, `kind = Ring` with `start_tick`. `mark_fs` animates its radius from
`ring_radius_px.0` to `.1` and its alpha down over the ping's ticks, from the uniform's presented tick, so a ping
needs no rebuild while it plays. A struck dot takes `attacked_color` while `tick − struck < flash_ticks`. That is
decided at rebuild time, because a rebuild happens on every presented tick.

### D-14. Where it sits in the HUD

The command panel's `columns_const` of three becomes a fixed-width left part and the three columns. The left part
is a square of `side_points` (172 of the panel's 180 points; S9's 291×286 is the same squareness). Its rectangle is
recorded as `HudGeometry::minimap`, and the image is centred in it. So the minimap is StarCraft II's bottom left,
the selection, queue and card keep their order to its right, and **the world rectangle does not change**: the panel
keeps its height, so every world-pixel GPU test, the frame budget's camera pose and the showcase framing stay as
they are. `HudLayout` gains `minimap: bool` (default `true`). The showcase, which leaves out the command panel
(T3), shows no minimap. A layout with `command_panel: false` and `minimap: true` draws the minimap alone in a
`side_points` bottom-left `egui::Area` that reserves no panel. No production profile uses that today; it is tested.

Under `BattleProfile::Sandbox`, the editor and the headless check, there is no `BattleFog` and no `BattleHudPlugin`
player systems, so there is no minimap, as for the attack snapshots.

## 8. Stop rules

1. **A world pixel or world rectangle changes** (M-11 or any existing GPU test that compares the world): stop. D-14
   is wrong, and the layout is amended before going on.
2. **M-S2 finds an allocation per frame** that the minimap adds and a retained buffer cannot remove (for example
   inside Bevy's extraction): stop and amend with the measured stack. The charter rule is not waived.
3. **M-B1 or M-B2 is exceeded by more than 2×**: stop and amend. The fallback is to rebuild marks only every second
   presented tick, as a recorded decision, never silently.
4. **The refactor of D-7 changes any existing world input test's assertion**: stop. The refactor must be pure.
5. **`rallies-plan` WP1 lands first and its `issue_pointer_order` shape conflicts**: rebase onto it. Stop only if
   `smart_plan` cannot take a `PointerTarget` built from a minimap aim. Its D-1 table takes a resolved target, so
   none is expected.
6. **The real shader or the real albedo arrays cannot be bound** (a validation error, a missing theme layer): fail
   loudly with the typed error and ask (charter §1.2). Never draw a flat colour in their place.
