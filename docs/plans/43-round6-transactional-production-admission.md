# Round 6: Transactional training admission and completion

Status: reviewed and approved by the orchestrator, including enqueue-time full supply reservation, captured supply-ledger migration and checksum schema3. Implementation is held during the coordinated workspace gate; preserve all existing dirty work, editor code and art assets.

## Goal and verified defects

Make a training command either admit one fully paid, authorized job or reject without changing authoritative state. A completed job must become exactly one legally placed entity, or remain intact while an ordinary exit obstruction is retried. Invalid live state must produce typed fatal evidence rather than swallowing a spawn error.

Current `SimWorld::process_commands` debits minerals/gas before locating an owned recipient, accepts any owned entity regardless of its authored training list, does not enforce completed/alive status or prerequisites, and ignores supply. The production phase pops a completed queue entry before attempting an unchecked spawn at `producer.position + (2,0)` and discards the result. The position may overlap the producing footprint, walls or another unit. `spawn_structure` advances `next_entity_id` before fallible placement, and both setup spawn methods wrap the ID counter. Failed placement can therefore change future IDs even though no structure exists.

The existing schema already contains `trains_units`, `requires_structures`, mineral/gas costs, supply consumption/provision and `u32` duration. These authored capabilities are currently bypassed. Some training lists name a dome product, apparently anticipating an unimplemented morph/build workflow. Do not turn that target into a mobile entity without footprint occupancy, remove its authored declaration, or invent morph behavior.

Supply fields currently have no lifecycle: `supply_used` starts at zero and `supply_max` at 200, and neither is maintained on spawn/death. Merely checking those stale fields would be an ineffective repair. Existing plan30 tests select actual producer/product pairs via `trains_units`, so they provide a useful behavioral migration base rather than a reason to keep arbitrary-recipient admission.

No exact SC2 frame/timer claim is added. ## Proposed supply policy and representation

**Approval decision:** reserve the product's full supply at enqueue, including waiting FIFO jobs. This is a conservative project rule that prevents simultaneous commands from oversubscribing the same capacity. It is not claimed to reproduce SC2's precise waiting-queue reservation timing. If a closer front-only reservation rule is required, approve a separate explicit `WaitingForSupply` state before implementation; do not accidentally switch policy during coding.

Replace the public tuple queue with a documented `ProductionJob { product: UnitTypeId, remaining_ticks: u32, reserved_supply: u32 }`. A zero remaining count means ready for placement, including a blocked ready job; the job remains at the front until successful commit. Supply is captured at acceptance so subsequent catalog corruption cannot retroactively free or charge resources. At completion the captured reservation must still agree with the supported product definition; a mismatched current cost is typed definition/invariant failure, not a fresh charge or silent reservation adjustment. Retain paid mineral/gas amounts only if a cancellation/refund feature is introduced later; this round has no cancellation operation and must not invent one via `Stop`.

Maintain player supply through successful entity lifecycle operations: `supply_used` counts living entity consumption plus accepted queue reservations; `supply_provided_total` stores the uncapped total from completed live providers; `supply_max` is the derived capacity capped at the existing standard 200 rule. Empty worlds begin with zero provided capacity, not a fictitious free 200. The uncapped total is necessary because removing a provider from a previously over-cap total may leave effective capacity unchanged. Keep the existing standard cap explicit/documented; alternate custom caps/race exceptions require a later authored rules policy, not unit-name special cases.

Setup spawn APIs are privileged setup operations: they may create an intentionally over-cap scenario without paying resources, but update the ledger exactly once. Gameplay enqueue cannot exceed `supply_max`. Successful training completion transfers its existing reservation to the live entity without double-charging supply. Killing a live entity releases its live usage and any reservations in its production queue; completed supply providers remove their uncapped contribution. Already admitted jobs retain their reservations if capacity is destroyed, and are not silently erased; new jobs fail while over cap. Destruction does not fabricate a cancellation refund.

All sums/differences use checked widened arithmetic and typed invariant errors; no saturating subtraction hides a corrupt reservation balance. Store enough ledger state to account for catalog-defined values consistently throughout the lifecycle. Freeze the accepted per-entity supply consumption/provision when spawning (or equivalently prohibit and detect changing those catalog values mid-world); do not subtract a newly edited cost at death. The recommended representation adds captured supply values to Entity and job state, with exact checksum coverage. Derivation/reconciliation tests must prove that the sum of live captured values and queue reservations matches the counters after each operation.

This intentionally changes initial supply presentation. Update timer fixtures to supply an actual provider or explicitly authored producer supply contribution. Preserve default scenarios' existing entities even if their initial roster exceeds their provided capacity; that is an authored scenario state, not permission to delete units or silently grant extra capacity. Report any initial over-cap scenario in acceptance notes. Public direct ledger mutation is unsupported unless an explicit checked setup API maintains the invariant.

## Completion and blocked-exit retry

Update only the front timer of a live, completed producer using the current lossless u32 countdown. An already dead producer does not finish a job before reaping; its outstanding reservations are released by the death transaction. Preserve phase ordering when a living producer completes first and is killed by the later combat phase in the same tick; do not retrospectively erase its committed child. At zero, stage completion by producer ID without popping the queue or spending another resource. Resolve a legal local exit using catalog radius/mover type, actual producer footprint and shared map clearance/traversal rules. Remove the fixed `+2` offset.

Use a deterministic finite perimeter of cell-center candidates immediately outside the producing footprint expanded by product clearance. Enumerate a documented clockwise order from the minimum coordinate; do not depend on hash iteration, wall clock, floats or randomized search. Ground candidates must satisfy terrain/structure clearance, ramp profile and map-boundary rules for the complete unit footprint. Air candidates obey their actual movement layer and map bounds, without inheriting ground obstacles. Reject occupied positions using the real collision radius and movement layer. Do not scan the entire map or jump across walls to make production succeed.

### Producer-boundary connectivity clarification

An individually clear candidate is insufficient. Ground production must establish a continuous local egress corridor from the actual producing footprint to that candidate. For a placed structure, derive the nearest boundary cell center by clamping the candidate cell coordinates to the inclusive placed-footprint rectangle; this selects an actual producer edge/corner, not an unrelated navigable cell. For a mobile producer, use its actual current center as the anchor and its occupied radius for candidate bounds. A dome archetype without a matching placed footprint is corrupt state, not permission to invent a foundation.

Validate the complete anchor-to-candidate segment through `GameMap::ground_segment_is_connected`. Terrain continuity is mandatory even inside the producing footprint and at its boundary: producer occupancy is the only exemption, never a waiver for cliff levels, malformed ramps, map boundaries or unpassable terrain. The step policy validates the product's entire swept grid footprint, including diagonal pinch cells, against real map occupancy and terrain. Only cells actually occupied by this producer ID may be exempted from structure occupancy during egress; every other structure, wall and terrain discontinuity blocks. The endpoint receives normal full clearance with no producer exemption, and collision admission excludes no other live unit. This describes emergence from the producer interior; it does not grant ordinary units movement through buildings.

Use the nearest boundary anchor and this direct finite corridor only. Do not search another disconnected island or move the anchor outside the footprint to make an obstructed exit pass. A larger product's wider candidate perimeter must still connect across every intermediate cell; a one-cell wall or cliff between producer and otherwise clear endpoint therefore blocks completion. Air products use the actual air layer's bounds and collision policy; ground topology is not imposed on them.

Regression fixtures must put a producer immediately beside a different-level clear plateau with no ramp, beside a narrow intervening real wall where a larger product's endpoint would otherwise appear clear, and beside a correctly oriented ramp landing. Verify disconnected endpoints remain blocked, genuine connected exits are accepted, and obstruction removal produces one entity without changing candidate order or bypassing the complete footprint check. For corner candidates, validate both terrain routes and diagonal occupancy pinches. This is a documented Space Scuffle egress rule, not an asserted SC2 exit-search algorithm.

A blocked local exit is a normal persistent game condition: keep the zero-tick job, its reservation and already-paid resources; do not allocate an entity ID, pop/refund/recharge, advance the next FIFO job, or report a fatal error. Retry once in the next normal production update. Expose a typed `ProductionBlock::ExitObstructed` in a derived per-producer observation buffer for UI/debugging. Reuse its storage each phase and let consumers inspect current status; do not emit a repeated error event every blocked tick. The observation is never consulted to decide placement: map/entities and the zero-tick job determine every retry.

Resolve ready producers in stable EntityId order. Candidate collision queries must include units committed earlier in the same phase; the ordinary target-acquisition spatial rebuild happens later, so the previous-tick grid alone is insufficient. Use a reusable production occupancy index refreshed once when ready completions exist and updated on each successful spawn, or a precisely documented shared-index update. No per-frame cloned world and no producer-by-all-units quadratic search. The existing shared spatial query truncates its radius to integer cells; production must pass an explicitly ceiled broad-phase envelope covering the product radius plus the largest relevant live collider radius, bounded by the map extent, and then use exact widened squared-distance comparisons. Do not inherit a fractional-radius omission or alter general steering behavior in this round. Validate radius representation before arithmetic, and test a fractional overlap straddling a bucket boundary. The derived obstruction observation does not enter canonical state because it is regenerated from the queue/map/entities and never drives decisions; queue reservation/countdown do enter the digest.

On success, prepare the entity/economy/ID mutation, commit one spawn and remove exactly the matching front job together. Unsupported/corrupt queued identity or arithmetic/ID failure is fatal evidence and preserves the job. Do not reinterpret a corruption failure as an ordinary blocked exit that retries forever. The second queued job receives no countdown during the same update, preserving plan30 behavior.

## Swept-footprint amendment: fractional producer origins

Review found that applying a radius-derived cell offset around only the DDA's current cell can miss an adjacent occupied cell when a mobile producer begins at a fractional coordinate near a cell edge. Cell-centered cardinal exits do not expose this. The orchestrator reviewed and approved the exact integer algorithm below; implementation and specified regressions are in progress.

The map-clearance footprint is the conservative axis-aligned square enclosing the product's circular collision radius, matching this round's discrete complete-grid-footprint admission. Live unit overlap still uses exact circle radii. For center segment `S→E` and positive Q32.32 radius `r`, enumerate only the integer cells within `[min(S,E)-r, max(S,E)+r]`; reject the query first if this swept rectangle exceeds actual map bounds. Cell indices use floor at the minimum and floor of `(maximum_raw-1)` at the maximum, so mere contact with an outer cell boundary does not add an occupied cell. Enumeration does not allocate and remains local to the finite egress corridor.

For each enumerated cell, determine whether its open interior intersects the swept square by intersecting the center segment with that cell's rectangle expanded by `r`. Use exact rational slab intervals: each axis contributes an entry and leave numerator over the positive absolute segment delta; a parallel axis requires strict interior membership. Intersect these intervals with `[0,1]` using signed i128 cross multiplication, with no floating point, sampling or division. An interval must retain positive length; a stationary center inside the expanded rectangle is accepted. Mere tangency to a wall is not overlap, consistent with circle-to-circle contact and the endpoint map-bound rule. This is exact swept-square/cell coverage, not a claim of exact circle/rectangle collision geometry.

Arithmetic invariant: let `M=i64::MAX`, `U=2^32`. After the full-radius map-bound check, all start/end raw coordinates lie between `r` and `M`, so each axis delta is at most `M-r`. Enumerated cell edges, including the one-cell west/south neighbors checked below, make each slab numerator's magnitude at most `M+r+2U`. Therefore every numerator×denominator comparison is bounded by `(M+r+2U)(M-r) <= M²+2UM < 2^127`. Coordinates, expansion and floor ranges are computed in i128 before narrowing validated nonnegative map-cell indices to u32. The private intersection helper is called only under this checked coordinate contract; boundary tests must exercise extreme representable origins/radii as well as ordinary small values.

Obstacle intersection is only one part of admission. First retain `ground_segment_is_connected` for the complete center path. For **every** swept footprint cell, require valid terrain self-connectivity and the normal occupancy policy (only actual cells owned by this producer may be exempt during emergence). For every west/south neighbor also intersected by the swept footprint, require `terrain_step_is_connected(neighbor, cell)`. This visits every undirected cardinal terrain edge once and rejects straddled cliff/ramp discontinuities. Positive footprint width includes corner-adjacent cells at a diagonal crossing; the center predicate additionally retains its explicit both-route diagonal terrain check. Endpoint validation repeats with no producer occupancy exemption. Thus no cell or edge gains a terrain waiver merely because its occupancy is exempted.

Required regressions: fractional mobile origin whose radius overlaps an adjacent real wall despite its center cell being clear; endpoint tangent versus one raw unit of overlap; forward/reverse and stationary slab cases; exact diagonal corner pinch; correctly oriented ramp acceptance and wrong orientation rejection; extreme valid coordinate products without panic; unchanged real wall-ring, large-product, map-edge, air-layer and two-producer contention tests. This amendment is reviewed and approved; acceptance still requires its specified behavioral and numeric regressions. No general movement or map-editor work is authorized by it.
