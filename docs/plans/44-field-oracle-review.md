## What the oracle must establish

The reference is the field generated by **all admitted, retained, clipped source edges**, with their actual birth times and cumulative actor-path coordinate. It does not reconstruct samples already evicted by the ring, smooth the trajectory, or replace emission with a hand-authored curve. Reduced controls approximate that specific source field. The source has up to127 positive/zero-measure edges from128 samples; this higher count is permitted in offline verification only. Production remains at most16 segment slots.

Keep six comparisons separate:

| Comparison | Inputs and result | What it can establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kernel identity | Exact mathematical special cases versus the independent CPU evaluator | Correct support, axial/radial normalization and nonnegative density |
| Reduction | Unreduced source versus selected reduced controls, before output rounding | Model error caused by geometric/attribute simplification |
| Packing | Reduced high-precision provenance versus exact returned/serialized `f32` fields | Error from positions, ages, phase, axes, lengths and inverse lengths |
| Field execution | CPU evaluation of those same packed values versus actual shipped WGSL helper/readback | CPU/GPU operation and binding agreement |
| Ray integration | Converged reference integral versus proposed bounded production steps | Quadrature/transmittance error, independently of reduction |
| Actual image | Real material, proxy/depth/view bindings and compositing versus oracle probes and fresh images | Integration and visual quality; requires separate coverage/sorting/performance evidence |

A small GPU/CPU difference cannot excuse a large source/reduced difference. A plausible image cannot excuse incorrect packet data. A source/reduced error must not be attributed to sampling until the same-field integration comparison isolates it. Preserve intermediate results rather than reporting only a final screenshot difference.

## Exact base kernel and units

For a nonzero segment with endpoints `p0,p1`, let `ell = |p1-p0|`, `d = (p1-p0)/ell`, `t = dot(x-p0,d)`, `v = x-p0-d*t`, and `u = clamp(t/ell,0,1)`. Coordinates, `ell`, radius and axial cap width are in world units. The actor radius is `r`; `h = 0.5*r` is common to every segment of that emitter. Keep the original actor-path coordinate separate from this segment's projected distance. Zero-measure segments contribute exactly zero and never evaluate an axis or reciprocal.

Define `S(z) = 0` for `z <= 0`, `S(z) = 3*z*z - 2*z*z*z` for `0 < z < 1`, and `S(z) = 1` for `z >= 1`. Then:

```
A(t; ell,h) = S((t+h)/(2*h)) - S((t-ell+h)/(2*h))
K(q) = (max(1-q*q,0))^2
q = |v| / R(u)
rho_j(x) = startup * A(t;ell,h) * K(q) * amplitude(u) * modulation_j(x,u)
rho(x) = sum_j rho_j(x)
```

This is the proposed summed segment field, not a nearest-segment field, maximum, mean, normalized blend or union-of-capsules distance function. The distinction matters at self-intersections and curved junctions. Density is dimensionless under this convention; a separate extinction coefficient has inverse-world-length units. An authored dimensionless extinction-per-radius parameter would need an explicit conversion `sigma_t = coefficient/r`. The existing ribbon's optical-depth multiplier cannot silently become a volume extinction coefficient without that unit decision.

At constant attributes and modulation1, `A` is nonnegative, has support `[-h,ell+h]`, and integrates along the infinite axis to `ell`. The radial integral is:

```
integral K(|v|/R) d(area)
  = 2*pi*R*R * integral_0^1 q*(1-q*q)^2 dq
  = pi*R*R/3
```

Therefore one segment's mass is `startup * amplitude * pi*R*R*ell/3`. This holds for short segments whose cap windows overlap as well as long ones; replacing the window difference with two independently multiplied cap fades changes the model and fails this identity. Summing mass over segments remains valid when their supports overlap, because the proposed field is a sum.

These identities are independent mathematical checks, not copied expected outputs from the implementation. Use them to verify the evaluator/integrator before studying approximation. At fixed age, inverse-square expansion amplitude `(R_base/R)^2` makes this cross-sectional mass proportional to `R_base^2`; it does not establish mass conservation for varying age, lifetime fade, changing support or reduced arc.

The support is a finite cylinder around an infinite-axis projection with axial fade, not a geometric capsule with hemispherical ends. Its boundary has vanishing density/first derivative under the proposed kernels and bounded smooth attributes. That does not prove the complete field is globally differentiable: clamping interpolated radius/age at segment endpoints can introduce derivative changes on interior endpoint planes. Test those planes explicitly; do not repeat a stronger smoothness claim than the formula supports.

## Attribute and authored-function decisions required before implementation

The existing plan specifies radius endpoints0.5r→0.75r, lifetime fade, bounded advected modulation and a minimum feature size, but not all their exact functions. The field implementation must not fill these gaps with unreviewed art constants. Freeze a versioned field specification before the evaluator and WGSL are implemented. It must name:

1. The exact age-to-radius law and interpolation order. A proposed linear law `R(a) = r*(0.5 + 0.25*clamp(a/lifetime,0,1))` matches the stated endpoints, but is a **review proposal**, not an existing shader contract. Choose whether to interpolate endpoint radii or derive radius from interpolated age; equivalence exists only where that chosen law is affine and unclipped. Record the choice, including cap regions where `u` is clamped.
2. The exact lifetime/head fade `F(age)`, with support, endpoint values/derivatives and parameters. Do not silently import the ribbon's fade formula or invent a new fade in the oracle. Define `amplitude = density_scale * F(age) * (R_base/R)^2` only once, with finite nonnegative validated inputs. The geometric startup factor uses the **clipped original emitted arc**, as already approved, rather than reduced chord sum.
3. The exact modulation function, bounds, generation seed, world/path/clock arguments, frequencies and advection units. A minimum feature size alone does not specify a function or prove the proposed ray step accurate. State whether modulation is evaluated separately per segment using its clamped phase or is a shared world-space factor; these are different fields in overlap regions.
4. The exact extinction and scattering/radiance model. Initial scalar-density tests use constant linear source color and extinction to isolate geometry, not fabricated production lighting. A volume has no existing ribbon surface normal. Any density-gradient normal or angular scattering term needs an explicit subsequent lighting contract; passing Stage2's renderer light binding does not choose one.
5. The exact packed-axis/length operation sequence. CPU reference uses exact serialized values for the packing comparison, including the packet's axis/inverse length; it must not recompute a cleaner axis and hide a mismatch. The ideal comparison separately uses the unrounded segment geometry.

Until these decisions are reviewed, the constant-attribute kernel suite is fully defined, but the complete age/modulation/color oracle is deliberately unadmitted. This is a specification dependency, not permission to ship a constant-density substitute. The field specification and every artifact record its version/hash.

## Junctions and the near-closed counterexample

For collinear, consistently oriented adjacent segments with **identical constant attributes**, the outgoing smoothstep of one window cancels the incoming smoothstep of the next. Their sum equals the window of the full segment. Test this exact identity for unequal segment lengths, very short segments and many subdivisions.

The cancellation is not a general subdivision-invariance proof. Even on a straight line, independently clamped interpolation of age/radius/phase makes the two segments evaluate different attributes on opposite sides of a shared endpoint's cap. The shared attributes agree exactly at the endpoint, but their overlap neighborhoods need not agree. With modulation depending on phase, the discrepancy can persist even if the geometric positions and source-arc scalar approximation are exact. Curved segments additionally have different axes and radial distances. Compare the full fields and their ray integrals; do not normalize the sum to suppress beads without a reviewed new model.

Required synthetic cases include straight constant attributes; straight linear age/path with varying radius; a right-angle turn; an acute turn approaching reversal; a gentle sampled arc; S bends; a true self-crossing; adjacent coincident points with distinct attributes; and an exact closed loop. Preserve both same-direction and opposing-direction segments. Test arbitrary rigid translation/rotation for the unmodulated ideal field and separately quantify loss of invariance from rounded inputs at large coordinates. World-anchored modulation is not expected to be translation invariant unless its declared coordinate frame is translated too.

Retain the independent example in radius-normalized coordinates:

```
source: 0 -> 0.04r -> 0.001r
source arc: 0.079r
reduced chord: 0.001r
middle position error at source-arc parameter: about 0.039494r
```

With constant attributes/modulation and the same original-arc startup factor, the reduced/source mass ratio is exactly `0.001/0.079 = 1/79`, a loss of78/79 (about98.73%). This is a mathematically defined failing **field-fidelity** example even though the current control approximation can pass. The oracle must expose it, including optically thin rays where saturation cannot conceal the loss. A saturated alpha image, an unchanged startup gain or a small positional error is not success. No automatic arc/chord density scaling or arbitrary new deficit threshold is authorized.

## Quantities to retain and optical comparisons

For each representation record density extrema with sample positions, nonfinite/negative counts, support occupancy, peak position, integral mass, first spatial moment/centroid when mass is nonzero, absolute field difference integral and normalized difference only when the reference denominator is nonzero. A zero source denominator is a separate case: report absolute spurious density/support rather than divide by a convenient epsilon. Signed mass difference alone is insufficient because spatially relocated density can conserve total mass.

For each declared world-space ray, retain the same support/depth-clipped interval and compute:

```
column_density = integral rho(x(s)) ds
optical_depth tau = integral sigma_t(x(s))*rho(x(s)) ds
transmittance T = exp(-tau)
alpha = 1 - T
```

For constant linear source color `c`, the analytic premultiplied result is `c*(1-T)`. A piecewise-constant production integration step of optical depth `dtau` contributes `T_before * (1-exp(-dtau)) * c`, then multiplies transmittance by `exp(-dtau)`. Splitting an exactly homogeneous interval must not change the ideal answer. Compare varying-color transport separately with a converged reference; do not average straight colors first and call it equivalent compositing.

Record `tau` and `T` as well as alpha/RGB, including optically thin, moderate and saturated conditions under the reviewed extinction parameters. Alpha saturation and display tone mapping can hide substantial field loss. The density oracle operates before exposure/tone mapping/dither. The later actual material path must retain Stage1's exactly-once premultiplication contract: accumulated premultiplied radiance requires recovery of straight color for non-HDR grading when alpha is positive, then one premultiplication; HDR stays linear. Zero-alpha output is zero RGB. No screenshot tolerance from the storage-equivalence round transfers automatically to this structural change.

Ray sets include broadside, end-on, oblique, bend-interior/exterior, self-crossing and tangent/support-boundary rays, with actual saved gameplay/close camera rays in addition to synthetic mathematical rays. Full rendered coverage at camera-inside, near/far cuts, orthographic views, nonzero viewports and opaque cliffs remains the separate approved proxy/depth suite; a successful ray integral cannot prove that the rasterizer invoked that ray.

## Temporal field comparison

Use consecutive actual admission frames at30/60/144Hz, including the real time/clock/speed semantics in plan44. For each frame construct source and reduced fields at that frame's immutable packet clock. Compare both absolute fields and the change in reduction error:

```
E_k(x) = rho_reduced,k(x) - rho_source,k(x)
extra_temporal_change = E_(k+1)(x) - E_k(x)
```

This separates changes already caused by the producer from changes introduced by selecting different controls. Evaluate on the union support in fixed world space; a second explicitly advected comparison may diagnose transport but must not replace the fixed-space result. For optical rays retain analogous changes in `tau`, transmittance and premultiplied radiance with a fixed camera and background. If the camera moves, label that separate sequence and retain its actual ray changes.

Mandatory transitions are first/second sample, very short history, turn onset, split insertion/removal and equal-score tie changes,8r support clipping through an edge, ring-capacity eviction with oldest sample still younger than fade, age expiry, stop beyond full fade, restart, disable/re-enable, owner retirement/reuse, and first scene return. Include an authored-motion frame that changes selected topology without a simulation discontinuity. Do not claim a smooth spatial cap prevents a temporal jump at eviction or split replacement. Compare the source field across such transitions too; an unacceptable source jump cannot be repaired or excused by matching it accurately.

Keep Blink separate: a bridge already present in the real presented/emitted source history is not introduced by reduction. Preserve its command/admission and displayed-pose evidence and block teleport-safe claims until the presentation-boundary issue is resolved. The field oracle must not secretly remove its points. During a retained upload, geometry ages, source clock and modulation seed remain frozen with that selected packet; only the separately approved current camera/view-light state may change. Do not age the CPU reference to a newer clock while testing an old selected GPU packet.

## Numerical evidence and acceptance without an invented fidelity threshold

There are three different decisions:

1. **Exact invariant checks.** Support-zero, nonnegativity, zero-measure behavior, constant-attribute telescoping, analytic mass and homogeneous optical transport have explicit mathematical expected values. Derive floating-point comparison bounds from the actual arithmetic and input conditioning; record operation order, precision and any subnormal/overflow conditions. An unexplained fixed epsilon is not an art policy. At exact support endpoints, test the specified branch values directly. Analytic identities are the authority for checking the numerical integration method, not its own output at another grid size.
2. **Numerical approximation resolution.** For variable fields, use a bounded adaptive reference integration with intervals split at known support/cap/interpolation boundaries. Retain successive refinements and error estimates for every reported integral, with a declared finite work cap and explicit `ReferenceNotConverged` result. Consecutive estimates agreeing is convergence evidence, not a certified interval unless a valid derivative/interval bound has actually been supplied for the chosen field. Do not label it certified otherwise. The source/reduced difference must be resolved relative to numerical uncertainty before drawing a conclusion. A grid can miss a thin lobe; include geometry-aware probes and support partitioning rather than only uniform voxel samples.
3. **Representation/art fidelity.** Report resolved absolute/relative changes and failure examples without introducing a new global arc-deficit or density-error cutoff in this document. The known78/79 loss must be identified as unacceptable fidelity, and exact invariants must pass. General approximation limits need explicit review using the measured corpus, thin-regime optical errors, temporal changes and actual visual requirements before a full field implementation is admitted. Until those limits/model choices are approved, a nonzero approximation is measured, not automatically accepted. Numerical convergence is not aesthetic acceptance.

The actual GPU helper test later uses the production WGSL field implementation loaded through the real asset path, real packed buffers and preserved adapter/binary/source identities. Compare on actual target hardware with the same rounded inputs. Document supported float/transcendental precision and conditioning when proposing its tolerance; no arbitrary portability claim or hand-written test shader that bypasses production math. Test storage layout and complete selected-packet bytes as well as results. Material specialization, raster coverage, MSAA/depth and screenshots still require actual pipeline execution afterward.

The reference runner may spend more CPU work than production, but every adaptive computation has a finite declared work cap and typed failure. Reference cost is never reported as production cost. The104×16 ceiling, large rectangle overlap risk and actual GPU budgets in plan44 remain unchanged. Neither this oracle nor successful reduction establishes spare GPU time.

### Age, expansion, fade and tint

For a segment, use the previously defined clamped geometric projection `u`, then:

```
a = mix(endpoint_age0, endpoint_age1, u)
z = clamp(a / lifetime, 0, 1)
R(a) = r * mix(radius_start_radii, radius_end_radii, S(z))
F(a) = S(z / head_rise_lifetime)
       * (1 - S((z - tail_fade_start_lifetime)/(1 - tail_fade_start_lifetime)))
startup = S(clipped_original_emitted_arc / (r * startup_length_radii))
expansion_amplitude = (r * radius_start_radii / R(a))^2
boost_amplitude = select(1, boost_density_multiplier, published_boosted)
```

At or beyond the lifetime the fade is exactly zero. A negative/nonfinite age is invalid packet input, not something the `z` clamp is intended to conceal. At age0 the head rise is zero; it reaches one smoothly rather than exposing a new opaque cap. Radius is derived **after** interpolating age, using the same function in source/reduced/packed references. Do not instead interpolate precomputed endpoint radii: the smoothstep radius law is nonlinear and the two operations differ.

Define `albedo_age = mix(cool_albedo,warm_albedo,warm_fraction*S(z))`, then mix toward `boost_albedo` with `boost_tint_fraction` only for the selected boosted owner. There is no new sinusoidal hue band and no additive emission term. This is a proposed restrained color hierarchy, subject to actual art rejection/iteration.

The complete segment contribution is:

```
rho_j = startup * A_j * K(|v_j|/R(a_j))
        * expansion_amplitude_j * F(a_j) * boost_amplitude * M_j
rho = sum_j rho_j
```

All terms are nonnegative under admission. For an actual production sample, compute the extinction-weighted color numerator `sum_j rho_j * albedo_j` alongside density. When density is positive, divide that numerator by density to obtain the sample's straight source albedo. This division mixes colors of overlapping material; it does **not** divide or normalize the density sum, repair mass loss, or suppress a junction's excess optical thickness. At zero density skip lighting/contribution and leave transmittance unchanged.

### Exact bounded modulation and phase coordinates

Use one product of three sine modes, not a noise-octave stack. Construct the orthonormal world basis `B = Ry(yaw) * Rx(pitch) * Rz(roll)` from authored radians at startup; its columns are `e1,e2,e3`. Record the exact resulting rounded basis in the packed oracle. With original interpolated actor-path coordinate `phi`, wavelength ratio `lambda`, path coefficient `beta`, and emitter-local packet clock `eta`, define:

```
q1 = dot(e1, x)/(lambda*r) + beta*phi/r - rate1*eta + seed_phase1
q2 = dot(e2, x)/(lambda*r)                 - rate2*eta + seed_phase2
q3 = dot(e3, x)/(lambda*r)                 - rate3*eta + seed_phase3
M  = 1 + strength * sin(2*pi*q1) * sin(2*pi*q2) * sin(2*pi*q3)
```

This fully specifies the field and its bounds; its aesthetic adequacy is unknown. The world basis does not turn with a segment, camera or actor. Only `phi` is segment-interpolated, with the same endpoint clamping as age. Seed phases are stable for the published owner/generation and change only on real reassignment. A concrete deterministic seed proposal is SHA-256 over version tag plus the canonical little-endian owner identity and generation; take three successive unsigned24-bit groups and divide each by2^24. Compute this once per owner assignment, never in a fragment or per-frame heap operation. The exact identity widths/encoding must match the actual published ownership type in the packet blueprint, not a truncated entity index.

Avoid evaluating sine on an ever-growing unwrapped world/time argument. The packet proposal would carry three wrapped phase offsets at its real geometric anchor and a path anchor. Evaluate bounded local `x-anchor` and `phi-path_anchor` terms in the shader, with the anchor contributions, rates and the same immutable clock folded into the wrapped phases on the CPU. This algebra preserves the declared world/path field; it is not a per-frame phase reset. The reference retains both the unwrapped provenance and exact packed wrapped values and tests anchor changes for continuity. Large-coordinate/long-clock phase reduction must have a measured representability check; merely using `f64` does not prove arbitrarily large phase arguments accurate. Reject unrepresentable input explicitly rather than use camera-relative reseeding or an arbitrary remainder that jumps.

The existing0.5r minimum-feature contract applies to the **combined** spatial phase, not just the authored wavelength. Inside a segment, the three dimensionless spatial frequency vectors are:

```
k1 = e1/lambda + beta * ((phi1-phi0)/ell) * d
k2 = e2/lambda
k3 = e3/lambda
```

The product expands into modes with vectors `+/-k1 +/-k2 +/-k3`. Require every such mode's norm to be at most2 cycles per radius, using the actual rounded inputs and operation bounds; in clamped cap regions the path derivative is zero. A small geometric chord carrying a large path-coordinate difference can violate this condition even with lambda2. Record that as a field-admission failure and review the representation, not as permission to clamp phase or erase a loop. This enforces the proposed modulation feature limit, not a new arc-deficit threshold.

That combined-sine bound applies only to modulation. Inside a short chord, the interpolated age gradient has magnitude `abs(age1-age0)/ell`; it can be steep even when every sine mode passes. Composing that age with the head/tail fade, expansion and tint can create much narrower density/color changes. The axial/radial kernels and multiplication of all factors add their own variation. Consequently, neither the0.5r body-radius minimum nor the combined frequency bound proves that0.125r steps or the104-step ceiling are accurate. Exact fade/modulation choices remain conditional on the planned same-field convergence tests, temporal comparisons, actual images and measured cost. A failure requires explicit model/sampling review; do not silently clamp the age gradient, enlarge the step budget or treat modulation admission as complete field admission.

### Junction behavior retained as a testable risk

This candidate intentionally retains the summed axial-window model and clamped per-segment interpolation; it does not add a hidden overlap normalizer or choose the nearest segment. At a shared endpoint the attributes agree, but surrounding cap neighborhoods can have different ages/radii/path phases. Thus only the constant-attribute case has the exact telescoping guarantee. The new radius law and modulation make the varying-attribute discrepancy explicit; they do not claim to remove it.

Before a GPU field is authorized, evaluate straight varying-age subdivisions, short-segment runs,90-degree/acute turns, reversals and genuine legal source/reduced traces with these **exact** functions. Retain peak density, mass, column density and temporal error fields. Inspect the unmodulated diagnostic too so modulation cannot hide a bead or split jump. If varying-attribute source segmentation itself produces unacceptable beads/discontinuities, reject this model and propose an explicit different attribute-transport/kernel formulation. A convolution over the centerline with attributes inside the integral could change subdivision behavior, but it would require a separate bounded evaluation/cost design; it is not silently introduced here.

### Volume angular illumination without a surface normal

Reuse Stage2's actual renderer `Lights` buffer, current dynamic view binding, extracted count and `skip` semantics. For each real ray, `v` points from the scattering point toward the viewer: it is the negative normalized world ray direction, including orthographic views. Each `l` is the renderer's normalized `direction_to_light`. No facing normal, density gradient, tangent-derived Lambert term or synthetic sun is used.

Use a Henyey–Greenstein-shaped response with both vectors pointing away from the scattering location. PBRT documents this direction convention and the corresponding positive cross term; it also identifies positive asymmetry with forward scattering. [PBRT, Phase Functions](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Volume_Scattering/Phase_Functions).

```
c = clamp(dot(l,v), -1, 1)
D = 1 + g*g + 2*g*c
P = (1-g*g) / (D * sqrt(D))
L_rgb = base_illumination_rgb
        + directional_weight * sum_eligible((light.color.rgb/reference_lux) * P)
```

`P` is4*pi times the normalized phase function: it has mean one over direction, so `g=0` gives exactly1 and preserves the existing dimensionless lux/reference scaling. This is an **artistic illumination response**, not calibrated physical radiance or a full radiative-transfer solution. The fixed authored fill is not Bevy ambient-light extraction. There is no directional shadow lookup, medium self-shadow, multiple scattering or emissive gas claim. Existing opaque scene depth clips the viewer ray but does not simulate light attenuation before the sample.

For positive `g`, a light behind the mist relative to the viewer has `l` opposite `v` and receives the larger response. With `|g| <= 0.5`, `D >= (1-|g|)^2 >= 0.25`; the normalized peak is `(1+|g|)/(1-|g|)^2 <= 6`. At the proposed `g=0.2`, the forward peak is1.875. These are derived bounds, not arbitrary intensity clamps. Clamp the dot only to the mathematical unit-vector range to handle normalization roundoff; invalid/nonfinite directions are still rejected by the binding/input contract.

All directional lights are spatially uniform, the ray direction is constant along a straight view ray, and `g` is an emitter style parameter. Therefore this angular light sum can be evaluated once for the ray and reused across its integration steps; no claim about measured GPU savings is made before implementation. The sample source color is `albedo_sample * L_rgb`. Integrate that with the real density using front-to-back optical transport. Retain Stage1's HDR/non-HDR output contract, and test current view lights changing while a geometry packet is held.

### Numerical envelope and integration ordering

The existing Stage2 per-channel admission `MAX/(4*N)` bounds a sum with Lambert factors at most1. It cannot be reused unchanged when the proposed angular response can reach6. For this candidate, define `P_bound = (1+abs(g))/(1-abs(g))^2` and derive a stricter per-light normalized-channel limit `MAX/(4*N*max(P_bound,1))`, with checked actual `f32` operation order and conservative rounding. Here `N` is the actual locked engine maximum directional-light count. Immutable reference/g ensure selected and retained packets share this envelope. Current-light changes still validate all rigs before mist extraction/queue; do not change unrelated camera rendering policy. This is a proposed **future field-specific admission amendment**, not a change to accepted Stage2 during the freeze.

With admitted base/color/directional weights at most1, this envelope leaves substantial accumulation headroom; prove/check the actual packed-light multiplication, phase computation, RGB sum and premultiplied accumulation with the chosen operation order. Do not rely solely on a rearranged `f64` inequality, and retain the scope limit: arbitrary view grading/output settings are not guaranteed finite by light admission. All real ordinary lights should be audited against the new bound before integration.

Density has a separate finite bound. With16 segments, startup/window/kernel/fade/expansion at most1, modulation at most1.5 and boost at most2, total density is at most48 in exact arithmetic. Evaluate per-step optical depth as `extinction_per_radius * rho * (ds/r)`, avoiding an unnecessary intermediate inverse-world-radius extinction that can underflow/overflow even when the dimensionless expression is representable. The proposed step interval is at most0.125r, so the authored extinction ceiling4 gives a maximum step depth24 before roundoff. Validate the actual rounded ratio and finite intermediate operations; no density clamp is inserted to force that bound.

For the initial oracle/material comparison, traverse the complete admitted interval. The existing plan allows early transmittance termination only after proving a remaining-contribution bound; this candidate's HDR source color may exceed1, so `T < 1/256` alone does not establish a one-code-value error. Any later early-exit optimization must use the actual source-color bound and output contract and receive separate verification. Keep the approved104-step ceiling and16-segment limit.
