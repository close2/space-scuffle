## Proposed rendering approach

### A density volume around the retained trajectory

Keep the proven distance/time history, generation publication, disabled/re-enabled semantics and persistent transport from plan 35. Represent the wake as a bounded, smoothly joined density field around the history centerline. Use an explicit small, curvature-aware set of control segments derived from the retained history; preserve the head, turns and age ordering. The source radius, downstream expansion, extinction, dispersion rate and restrained tint are validated generic catalog fields, independent of creature identifiers.

The density field must approach zero smoothly at its spatial support, age limit and tail. It has an asymmetric dense near-source body, lower-density surrounding flow and downstream breakup; it must not have equal-width parallel bands. Advected three-dimensional density detail is evaluated in world/path coordinates with continuous visual time. Owner generation supplies a stable seed, not a mutable global emission counter. Expansion reduces density rather than increasing total opaque mass. Turning deforms the centerline itself, and stopping ages existing material without attaching new material to a stationary actor.

This is a procedural rendering model for a gas-like effect, not fabricated creature animation or substitute media. All creature/building media continue through the exact production admission path.

### Exact bounded control reduction and finite support

Keep the existing owner/history clocks and sample order for the first structural implementation. The volume may shorten visible support before the age limit, but must not pretend that the fixed ring preserved the whole lifetime. Copy retained sample references into preallocated scratch, remove only exactly coincident position/age entries, and clip the chronological polyline to an authored maximum **8 rendered radii of actual emitted-centerline arc length** by interpolating the cut point. Measure this arc after the 0.7-radius emission offset; do not assume the observed actor's cumulative distance bounds a turning offset curve. If the retained ring is shorter, its oldest point is the explicit support boundary. Preserve every original birth time and coherent path coordinate used in interpolation. Zero/one-point histories do not draw a puff.

Reduce to at most 17 control points / 16 segments with a bounded, iterative polyline simplifier. Always retain the oldest/newest support endpoints. For each candidate segment, parameterize source points by cumulative emitted-centerline arc length, interpolate position and birth time, and compute `max(position_error/(0.05*r), birth_error/(lifetime/64))`. Split the segment at the greatest score above one, breaking equal scores by chronological source index; repeat until all scores are at most one. No recursive heap allocation, unordered traversal or frame-dependent random choice is used. Fixed scratch holds at most the validated source sample capacity (currently 48, maximum 128) and 17 selected indices. Worst-case reduction work is bounded by source capacity times segment capacity, rather than an unbounded iterative fit.

The 0.05-radius positional tolerance is one tenth of the proposed minimum body radius; the temporal tolerance limits interpolation error to 1/64 of the fade lifetime. These are approximation contracts to verify against actual 30/60/144 Hz trajectories, not magic visual-quality scores. With the radius-0.375, world-radius-1 circular baseline, a 0.01875-world-unit chord-error tolerance admits approximately 0.388 radians per segment; a 1.35-second, 2.5-unit/s arc needs about nine segments, leaving capacity for endpoints/turn detail. This calculation explains the initial 16-segment budget; it is not proof for arbitrary paths.

If reduction needs a seventeenth segment, return `ControlBudgetExceeded` with owner generation, source count and measured maximum error. Do not silently relax tolerance, drop a significant corner or render a straight shortcut. The normal game/capture error path must report the failure explicitly. Before shipping, adversarial legal motion tests and real full-battle sequences must demonstrate that the selected budget is adequate. If ordinary motion fails, revise this blueprint's representation/capacity and remeasure; the error is an admission guard, not an acceptable normal gameplay behavior.

Use a finite-support capsule-like field for each segment. Store its unit axis and length reciprocal. For a point, project onto the infinite axis, compute radial distance and clamp the interpolation parameter only when interpolating endpoint age/radius/path coordinate. The radial kernel is `(max(1-q*q,0))^2`, where `q=radial_distance/radius`. The axial weight is `smoothstep(-h,h,t) - smoothstep(length-h,length+h,t)`, with `h=0.5*r`. For collinear adjacent segments with equal attributes, these axial windows telescope at the shared endpoint instead of doubling density. Curved junctions may overlap as a continuous density sum; validate that they do not form bright beads. The field is zero with zero first derivative outside its support, so neither a mesh end nor a missing older sample makes a rectangular silhouette.

Proposed authored body radius expands from 0.5r to 0.75r with age. Density is reduced by the inverse square of that expansion, preserving approximate cross-sectional mass rather than making the widening tail opaque. Multiply by the lifetime envelope and a startup-length envelope `smoothstep(0,1,L/r)`, where L is the retained centerline length. The axial cap is already smooth at both ends. Stopping only advances the stored ages; it must not grow a new stationary source. A clipped or evicted oldest boundary is tested as a moving smooth cap, including consecutive eviction frames; passing a static field plot cannot establish temporal continuity.

Three-dimensional density modulation uses one analytic, band-limited advected field with its smallest authored feature at least 0.5r, bounded positive modulation and a seed derived from the published owner generation. It modulates the continuous body rather than generating independent sphere particles. Do not add progressively smaller octaves until numerical aliasing or glow happens to conceal the silhouette. Advection coordinates are published path distance, world position and the selected packet clock; they do not reset on camera motion. The art iteration may change validated modulation strength, tint and expansion within this representation, with complete captures after each change.

### Ray integration, culling and a derived step bound

Render the sole per-view conservative screen rectangle specified in the coverage section below. Reconstruct the world-space ray from the actual inverse view/projection and physical viewport coordinates; use the proper perspective or orthographic origin. Intersect selected support with a robust slab test including parallel axes, then clip the interval against the actual near/far planes and opaque scene depth. Use depth at the correct physical viewport offset and current MSAA sample, with reverse-Z conventions verified against the real shader helpers. Use an always-pass proxy depth comparison and no depth writes; reject empty or fully occluded intervals in the fragment. This preserves true depth occlusion while decoupling proxy raster coverage from any surviving AABB exit face. Actual cliff/foreground tests must demonstrate that contract.

The 8r centerline arc and at-most-0.75r radial field with 0.5r axial caps bound the proxy diagonal conservatively by `8r + 2*sqrt(3)*(0.75r+0.5r) < 12.34r`. Include the 0.05r reduction-error expansion in culling only; the selected analytic field itself uses the reduced segment support. Validate an upper support diagonal of **13r**. With the smallest density feature 0.5r and four integration samples per feature, target step length is **0.125r** and the hard maximum is therefore **104 steps** (`ceil(13/0.125)`). This ceiling follows from the support/feature contracts. Do not claim an arbitrary 16-step loop is sufficient for a camera looking along the entire wake. Reject a packet whose finite support exceeds the validated bound; do not silently increase step length.

For each actual clipped ray interval choose `n=ceil(interval_length/(0.125r))`, clamped below only to one and checked above against 104; use equal midpoint samples with `ds=interval_length/n`. A typical side view through 1.5r thickness uses about 12 steps, while end-on views may reach the cap. The shader loop is statically bounded at 104 with an early break at n. Each sample checks at most 16 segments and sums their finite-support density. No independent per-segment raymarch is used, and no per-fragment dynamic array is required. Zero-density samples do not run unnecessary lighting work. Do not use temporal jitter/reprojection in this first implementation; their ghosts and history ownership would require another design.

Integrate optical thickness `a=1-exp(-sigma*density*ds)` and premultiplied front-to-back color. The finite support and per-unit-distance extinction make opacity approximately invariant to numerical step subdivision. Stop when transmittance is below 1/256 only after validating that the bounded remaining contribution is below one output code value for the declared color range; otherwise traverse the full bounded interval. Compare production 0.125r steps against a test-only 0.0625r reference at matched states and grazing angles. Proposed acceptance: alpha absolute error below 0.01 and linear premultiplied RGB error below 0.02 for non-edge samples, with separately inspected edge differences. Tighten step/support design if actual captures show bands or flicker despite those numeric bounds.

The field's smallest feature and cap smoothness are part of validation. If art requests finer detail, update the sampling bound and measured GPU budget before changing the field. A view-specific lower sample count is not introduced merely because the actor is small; undersampling a small dense feature can create unstable brightness at gameplay distance.

Lighting remains restrained single scattering plus ambient tint, not additive emission. Sum only the eligible actual directional lights, with a validated angular phase response and explicit reference-illuminance scale; preserve their linear colors. No opaque mist shadow is cast and no unreviewed self-shadow simulation is claimed. Proper light and output-transform contracts are independently verified below before visual tuning.

### Per-view clipped-slab rectangle coverage

Use one view-dependent conservative screen rectangle per emitter. This is the sole proxy path for the proposed volume technique, not a cheap fallback. Each slot owns four immutable corner identifiers and six indices. The vertex shader fetches the selected packet's world AABB and the current view's actual frustum/projection. It clips that AABB to the current near/far depth slab before projecting it. The four rectangle vertices use a valid fixed clip-space depth (0.5 at W1); true entry/exit and opaque occlusion remain determined by analytic ray/AABB/scene-depth clipping in the fragment. No proxy depth writes and no shell-dependent shortcuts are introduced.

For the supported perspective and orthographic cameras, near and far planes are parallel. A box clipped by those two planes has vertices only among its eight original corners and intersections of its twelve edges with either plane: at most32 candidates. Keep original corners inside both planes. For each edge-plane crossing, retain its finite intersection only if it also lies within the other plane. Project retained candidates with the actual `clip_from_world`, take NDC XY extrema, conservatively expand by one physical pixel, and intersect with the current viewport rectangle. An empty candidate set or empty rectangle produces a degenerate no-coverage proxy. Test the actual `View.frustum` plane ordering/sign from locked Bevy before implementation; do not assume that reverse-Z projection coefficients alone contain the camera's configured far culling plane.

The coverage argument is explicit: the clipped box is convex; for perspective it lies in front of the validated positive near distance, and projection of its edges is linear-fractional with positive W, whose extrema occur at endpoints. Projected convex-polytope extrema therefore occur among those finite candidates. Orthographic W is constant and the same bound follows from linear projection. If the camera is inside the box, clipping still produces the near/far intersection polygon, so the rectangle covers the relevant viewport even when all original exit faces would have been far-clipped. No face needs to survive rasterization. Camera-plane intersections are handled before homogeneous division, avoiding behind-camera or W0 projection. Validate supported finite projection/plane inputs at extraction. Perspective requires positive near distance; orthographic projection permits finite negative near distance with constant W1 and ordered near/far planes. Malformed or missing inputs fail explicitly. Use scale-aware dot-product roundoff bounds for half-space inclusion, plus outward pixel expansion, rather than a world-scale magic epsilon.

The fragment reconstructs its ray using the actual physical viewport origin and dimensions, clips the ray against the same near/far slab and selected world support, then against actual opaque depth at that pixel/sample. This explicitly handles finite far planes even for Bevy's infinite reverse-Z perspective matrix. Offscreen rectangles are clamped per view. Multiple cameras receive independently projected rectangles and independent ray intervals; no view's rectangle or camera-inside decision is reused by another. The packet anchor still controls transparent sorting independently of this screen-space coverage geometry, preserving plan35's selected-packet ownership and ordering contracts.

Cost is bounded: four static vertices evaluate at most32 candidates each, six indices per emitter, with no per-frame mesh creation and no screen-size-dependent CPU work. Record rectangle-covered pixels and actual fragment/ray-step distributions. Large rectangles while close to/inside a volume are real workload, not hidden by culling claims; the provisional GPU budget must hold in those cases or the design is rejected. This coverage policy does not solve translucent shell/volume sorting, which retains its separate overlap acceptance requirement.

Required real coverage tests compare actual rasterized coverage with independently computed ray/AABB intersections at pixel centers, including perspective and orthographic cameras, camera outside/inside support, near-plane cuts, far plane strictly inside the box, both planes cutting a large box, off-axis/rolled camera, nonzero viewport origin, differently sized simultaneous views, and an opaque cliff cutting the valid ray interval. Every analytically nonempty pixel must receive proxy coverage (allow conservative excess that the fragment rejects). Capture near/far crossing sequences, not only static masks. Run MSAA1/4 and retain depth-occlusion images. Full volume implementation remains held for approval after plan35's real cost comparison; this section records the approved coverage architecture only.

Locked-source confirmation for this proposal: Bevy0.15.3 `primitives/mod.rs::Frustum` constructs near at `half_spaces[4]` and custom far at `[5]`; `intersects_obb` uses inward signed half-space distance. `view/mod.rs::prepare_view_uniforms` copies these actual normalized planes into `View.frustum`, retaining the configured finite far plane even though `PerspectiveProjection::get_clip_from_view` uses `perspective_infinite_reverse_rh`. Missing frusta become zero planes in Bevy, so this technique must reject that missing-view contract explicitly rather than silently assume an infinite volume. Positive near distance is required for perspective projection; orthographic cameras may have a finite negative near distance because W remains1, provided near/far are ordered. These are distinct validated projection contracts.
