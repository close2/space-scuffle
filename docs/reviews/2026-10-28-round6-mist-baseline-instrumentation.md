### Screenshot evaluation and limitations

I opened native 1280×720 middle images from both zero-wake and both 400-wake runs. All actor rows are inside the image with clear edge margins; the prior bottom-row clipping is absent. The actual capture assertions report `(400,0)`, `(400,100)` or `(400,400)` actor/active-ribbon frustum counts as appropriate, and zero pool overflows. This combines projected bounds with inspected images; it does not claim that every tiny or overlapping wake is independently distinguishable by eye.

The 400-wake images show faint circular strips/rails around actors, especially against the dark cliff face, while many wakes disappear against the pale upper deck. They do not read as a coherent mist volume and do not establish intuitive motion direction at crowd scale. Art remains rejected against plan 44's rubric. The terrain/actor media and background also retain previously recorded quality limitations.

Within each run all three PNG digests are identical, as expected because the recorder freezes presentation time before screenshot readback. They prove callback completion, not temporal quality. The end times differ between runs (9.333–12.933 virtual seconds), since actual ten-second measurement completes after different frame counts. These are fixed-scene throughput artifacts, not equal-time image-equivalence pairs. The replacement's separate deterministic close/gameplay/turn/stop captures must use aligned presentation times, and equal-time crowd comparisons must not subtract these differing-phase images.

This baseline substantiates the approved persistent-storage work: remove repeated CPU geometry and dynamic mesh extraction while preserving current appearance and behavior. It does not authorize declaring the final game fast or visually acceptable. The combined workspace gate and orchestrator review precede replacement implementation.
